How the moment stays blind

Privacy is a property of the server, not the screen. Three guarantees, enforced in the backend.

Anonymous creator

The creator’s id never leaves the server. The client only gets a private “is this me?” boolean.

isCreator: boolean
No live tally

Counts are returned while collecting, never the voter set. During the moment, nothing about others.

respond() → { recorded }
Silent fizzle

Under quorum, the plan flips to fizzled on the next read and drops out of every dashboard.

revealGoing()
No scheduler A plan settles its own phase lazily - the next time anyone reads it. The privacy boundary lives inside the server’s procedures.

Why this stack

tRPC + Zod
@bethere/shared

One schema is the single source of truth. A change that breaks the client fails at compile time, not in front of a user. (vs REST + hand-written types, which drift apart.)

Expo / RN
iOS · Android · web

One codebase ships to all three. The web target is exactly what made the no-download onboarding possible. (vs native iOS/Android - double the code, no web.)

Postgres + Drizzle
typed SQL

The privacy guarantees - no names, blind tally - live inside the queries, server-authoritative and out of reach of the client. (vs Firebase - less control over the privacy logic.)

Lazy resolve
no scheduler

Phases settle on read - no background job to drift out of sync, and the privacy boundary stays in one place. (vs a cron scheduler - more to break, same result.)

Built for the group

A plan only resolves if the whole group acts - so the interactions are between people, and they feel immediate.
