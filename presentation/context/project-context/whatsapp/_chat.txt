[20/05/2026, 11:41:22] Nelson Gong: Skeleton — bold lines are the things you must get to; everything underneath is a probe to use if the answer is thin, not a script.0. Warm-up (~2 min)

Who's in your main friend group? How do you typically keep in touch?
(You're not collecting data here — you're getting them comfortable and gathering the cast of characters you can refer back to.)
1. The central excavation: the last failed plan (~8–10 min — spend the budget here)

"Tell me about the most recent time you tried to see friends and it didn't happen. Walk me through it from the first message."
Probes — use what's needed, in any order:

Who proposed it? What exactly did they say?
What did you reply, and why that?
Was there a "maybe" or a soft response from anyone? What was the exact wording?
When you saw that, what did you assume the person meant?
What did you do next? What did others do next?
When did you personally decide it wasn't happening — and what told you that? Was there a moment, or did it just drift?
Did anyone ever say "no"? Or did it just go quiet?
How did you feel about it afterwards? Did you bring it up later?


This single excavation tests cascade, silence-decay, and default-failure simultaneously. If you get nothing else from the interview, get this.2. The "maybe" mechanism (~4 min)

"When you send a 'maybe', what do you actually mean by it — in your head?" (probe for a percentage if they're up for it: "if you had to put a number on it — how likely?")
"And when you receive a 'maybe' from a friend, what do you do with it?" (same probe — what % do they read it as?)
The gap between these two answers is your evidence. If they show the asymmetry unprompted, that's quotable gold.
3. The polite veto (~4 min)

"Tell me about the last time someone suggested something you weren't keen on, but everyone else seemed into it. What did you actually say?"
Probes:

What stopped you from saying you weren't keen?
What did you think would happen if you did?
Did the plan happen? Did you go? How was it?


Mirror: "And the last time you proposed something and got a lukewarm response from someone — what did you read into it?"
4. Initiator cost (~3 min)

"Who's usually the one who organises in your group?"
Probes:

How does that feel for them? (or for you, if it's them)
Have you ever wanted to suggest something but didn't actually send the message? What stopped you?
The last time a plan worked out — who made it happen? What did they do?


5. Latency / risk-free intent — kill-or-confirm (~3 min)Without describing anything:

"Have you ever wanted to suggest meeting up but didn't? What stopped you?"
That's it. Don't lead. If they volunteer "I didn't know if anyone else was free / would want to" unprompted, the private-bid mechanism has legs. If they say "I forgot / couldn't be bothered", a different design wins. Let the answer guide you, not the other way round.6. Wrap (~2 min)

Anything you wish worked differently about how your group makes plans?
Anyone else in your group I should talk to? (snowball — your sample multiplier)
Only now, if asked, you can briefly describe what you're exploring — never before.
