The problem

Plans die in the gap between “we should meet up” and an actual time and place. Nobody wants to be the organiser, the effort is asymmetric, and a public maybe lets everyone hedge - so the plan quietly dies in a silent chat.

Opportunity · how might we

Help a group of friends turn a loose intention to meet into a committed plan - without anyone being the organiser, and without the social pressure of a public “maybe”.

So we built
BeThere

One person sends one plan. The group decides. No organiser, no maybe.

The group sees the plan - never who proposed it. No names, just the group.

How the moment stays blind

Privacy is a property of the server, not the screen. Three guarantees, enforced in the backend.

Anonymous creator

The creator’s id never leaves the server. The client only gets a private “is this me?” boolean.

isCreator: boolean
No live tally

Counts are returned while collecting, never the voter set. During the moment, nothing about others.

respond() → { recorded }
Silent fizzle

Under quorum, the plan flips to fizzled on the next read and drops out of every dashboard.

revealGoing()
No scheduler A plan settles its own phase lazily - the next time anyone reads it. The privacy boundary lives inside the server’s procedures.

Why this stack

Chosen for capability, not familiarity - every choice earns its place.

tRPC + Zod
@bethere/shared

One schema is the single source of truth. A change that breaks the client fails at compile time, not in front of a user. (vs REST + hand-written types, which drift apart.)

Expo / RN
iOS · Android · web

One codebase ships to all three. The web target is exactly what made the no-download onboarding possible. (vs native iOS/Android - double the code, no web.)

Postgres + Drizzle
typed SQL

The privacy guarantees - no names, blind tally - live inside the queries, server-authoritative and out of reach of the client. (vs Firebase - less control over the privacy logic.)

Lazy resolve
no scheduler

Phases settle on read - no background job to drift out of sync, and the privacy boundary stays in one place. (vs a cron scheduler - more to break, same result.)

Built for the group

A plan only resolves if the whole group acts - so the interactions are between people, and they feel immediate.

User to user
Anonymous voting - the group +1s times and activities together
The blind moment - everyone commits in the same window
“I’ll go if” - your answer depends on other people’s
Plus-ones - bring a guest into one meetup, across groups
Shared editing - any member can fix a live plan
Responsive
Instant +1 - optimistic UI; momentum moves as you tap
Conflict-safe edits - compare-and-set; a stale edit reports a conflict, never clobbers
Settles on read - no waiting on a background job
Honest gap: real-time push is next - today the nudge is device-local.

What changed for them

We set out to make the whole group behave like the rare Decisive One.

The Reluctant Initiator Floats a plan without it being her ask - no one to chase, no one to blame.
The Hedger No maybe to hide in; a deadline closes it before second thoughts grow.
