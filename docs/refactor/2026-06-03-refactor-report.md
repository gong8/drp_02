### 1. Consolidate date/time/string formatting into one shared home

`consolidate-datetime-string-formatting` · **Formatting & shared utils** · effort **S** · risk **low** · impact **med**

**Files:** `apps/mobile/src/lib/format.ts`, `apps/mobile/src/ui/DateTimeField.tsx`, `apps/mobile/src/ui/DateTimeField.web.tsx`, `apps/mobile/src/screens/CreateWizard.tsx`, `apps/mobile/src/screens/FloatBoard.tsx`

The forward "Date -> picker-string" conversion (a local-time `YYYY-MM-DD` / `HH:mm` builder plus its own `pad`) is triplicated across `DateTimeField.tsx`, `DateTimeField.web.tsx`, and `CreateWizard.tsx`'s `splitIso`, even though `apps/mobile/src/lib/format.ts` already owns the exact inverse (`isoFrom`) and almost every other date label helper. Separately, a "Wed 4 Jun" short-day label (`toLocaleDateString(undefined, {weekday:"short", day:"numeric", month:"short"})`) is duplicated byte-for-byte in `FloatBoard.dayLabel` and `DateTimeField.displayValue`. This epic moves the single source of truth into `lib/format.ts`: add `dateStringFrom(d)`, `timeStringFrom(d)` (and an optional `splitIso(iso)` built on them) next to `isoFrom`, and a separate `shortDayLabel(d)`. Then route the three forward-builders and the two Intl day labels through them. Mobile-only, behavior-identical, no `packages/shared` or `apps/api` changes.

**Why it matters:** `lib/format.ts` is already the documented home for these conversions and owns `isoFrom`; the comment in DateTimeField.tsx even says "Output strings match `isoFrom` in lib/format", yet the matching code lives three files away in two slightly different pad idioms (`n < 10 ? \`0${n}\`` vs `padStart`). That split is exactly the kind of drift that bites: if the picker-string contract ever changes, three copies plus two Intl labels must change in lockstep or the UI desyncs from `isoFrom`. Co-locating the forward converter beside its inverse makes the round-trip contract auditable in one place, removes three private `pad` copies and two duplicate Intl calls, and is a pure, byte-identical refactor (the differing pad idioms produce the same output for the 0-99 values involved).

### 4. Dedupe reconcileFloat's two argmax-with-earliest-created-tiebreak loops

`dedupe-reconcilefloat-argmax-tiebreak` · **API router dedup & structure** · effort **S** · risk **low** · impact **med**

**Files:** `packages/shared/src/logic/reconcile.ts`

reconcileFloat (packages/shared/src/logic/reconcile.ts) hand-codes the same "tally distinct backers, sort slate by createdAtMs ascending, scan and pick the max replacing only on strictly-greater count" algorithm twice - once for the winning idea (lines 59-74) and once for the best time (lines 82-95). Around each copy sits a duplicated ReconcileVote -> CandidateReactionInput adapter (filter to known ids + rename suggestionId to candidateId) and a duplicated tallyCandidates(...) -> Map indexing step. On top of that, the two votes.filter() pre-filters (ideaIds.includes at line 61, timeIds.includes at line 84) do dead work: tallyCandidates already zero-fills exactly the ids it is given and ignores reactions to unknown ids, so it is the sole gatekeeper - the .includes() guards change nothing. Collapse all of this into ONE file-local pure helper, pickByBackerCount, that subsumes adapter + tally + Map index + argmax + backer materialization for both axes; behavior is identical and the reconcileFloat signature stays untouched.

**Why it matters:** reconcileFloat is the core convergence rule - the function that turns a float into a moment, a collecting round, or a fizzle - and the earliest-created tiebreak is a load-bearing invariant tested explicitly ("breaks an idea tie toward the earliest created", "breaks a time tie toward the earliest created"). Today that invariant lives in two separate copies, so any future change (or a future Bedrock reconciler swap behind this signature) has two places to keep in lockstep and one place to get subtly wrong; an off-by-one between strictly-greater and greater-or-equal in just one copy would silently flip a tiebreak. Consolidating to a single helper makes the tiebreak, the vote->reaction adapter, and the tally indexing single-sourced, and deletes two redundant .includes() pre-filters that do real per-vote work for no behavioral effect (tallyCandidates already ignores unknown ids and zero-fills the supplied slate). Net: fewer lines, one place to reason about correctness, identical output.

**Change steps:**

1. Add a file-local (non-exported) generic helper to reconcile.ts: pickByBackerCount<T extends { id: string; createdAtMs: number }>(items: T[], candidateIds: string[], votes: ReconcileVote[], userFilter?: (userId: string) => boolean): { item: T; backers: string[] } | null.
