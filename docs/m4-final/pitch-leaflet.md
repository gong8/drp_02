# BeThere

No organiser. No maybe.

BeThere turns a vague "we should hang out" into a firm plan, with no organiser to chase and no public "maybe" to hide behind.

## Who it's for

Friend groups of about four to six - the most common group size in our survey - who coordinate in a group chat and routinely watch plans fizzle. In our research, larger groups were harder to pin down, not easier, so BeThere is built for the awkward, mid-sized group where one early "maybe" can sink the whole plan.

## The problem

Plans die in the gap between "we should meet up" and an actual time and place. Nobody wants to be the organiser, the effort is asymmetric, and a public "maybe" lets everyone hedge. One early hesitation makes the group's certainty collapse, and the plan quietly dies in a silent chat. In our survey of 43 people, 86% said that when they are only slightly unsure they send a "maybe" or nothing at all, rather than a clear yes or no.

How might we help a group turn a loose intention to meet into a committed plan, without anyone being the organiser, and without the social pressure of a public "maybe"?

## How it works

1. Anyone sends one meetup to the group - a few candidate times, maybe an activity - sent anonymously. The group sees the plan, never who proposed it.
2. Everyone votes on the times and activities. Momentum is visible (how many want each option), but who voted is never shown.
3. At a deadline, the top time and activity win automatically. A blind, timed moment opens: each person replies Yes, Can't make it, or "I'll go if [people]". No live tally to second-guess.
4. It clears ("you and four others are in") or it quietly fizzles - no awkward dead plan left in the chat.

## Before and after

Before: a group chat with "we should do something soon!!", forty messages, a few maybes, one early "I can't", and a plan that dies in silence while everyone waits for someone else to lead.

After: a firm time and place, real commitment, no organiser, and no public maybe. If it does not come together, it disappears quietly - no blame, no dead thread.

## Why we built it this way

Grounded in real research: a 43-response survey and interviews across three rounds with friend-group planners. The headline finding - 86% of people respond to mild uncertainty with a "maybe" or silence - named the enemy. One interviewee, Tom, argued for the core mechanic in his own words: "the organisers should have an option to be anonymous... nobody likes being the one to push things... it's more of a consensus than one person giving a proposal." BeThere makes that the default for everyone.

BeThere - be there, without the organiser.
