# DRP - Assessment Template (2026): Designing for Real People

> Claude-readable conversion of `Assesment_Template_2026.pdf` (root of repo).
> Projects milestones & assessment criteria. Each rubric below runs from the
> weakest level (left) to the strongest (right). Where the source used unlabelled
> columns, they are mapped to **Level 0 / 1 / 2 / 3** (0 = worst, 3 = best).

## Milestone 0: Pre-pitch project framing (not graded)

> "Getting off to a good start. What is your project about and why does it matter?"

Choose a project area you're inspired by, passionate about, and where you have
access to the people involved. As a group, prepare for the Milestone 1 elevator
pitch. Use the DRP Playbook / Week 1 to clearly articulate what you're focusing
on and how much you understand the context and problem.

- **The context and knowledge base** - Your area of interest and focus, e.g. food
  charity (the place, the people, the story). What knowledge and expertise do you
  have to work on this project? What access to users/stakeholders do you have to
  test and develop ideas? What potential ideas and resources will help you develop
  a meaningful digital touchpoint?
- **The people** - Specific details around your target audience and other
  stakeholders connected to the challenge area, e.g. food charity worker,
  volunteers. The richer and more detailed the personas, the better.
- **Evidence** - Tangible "data" gathered as the basis for your project. Data in
  design terms is qualitative, preferably first-hand: think people, not numbers.
  This leads to authentic, rich insights and usually only requires a few
  well-chosen, relevant people.
- **Insight and analysis** - The specific observations and meaning drawn from the
  evidence; the key statements and research based on your data. The people you're
  designing for are usually not consciously aware of these until you present them.
- **Problem Statement** - Your challenge statement based on the above evidence.
  e.g. "Hundreds of thousands of tonnes of good food is wasted by the UK food
  industry every year. At the same time, millions of people are struggling to
  afford to eat. This food charity addresses these 2 issues by redistributing food
  industry surplus... However, project managers are struggling to effectively
  manage the supply and demand of these volunteers."
- **"How Might We..." question** - A succinct description of the challenge you will
  address. e.g. "How Might We... enable food charity project managers to strengthen
  and efficiently organise their volunteers in order to create a vibrant and
  reliable volunteer network whilst reaching increasing numbers of people in need."
  Some early ideas may emerge towards a digital touchpoint.

Week 1 Clinic Hours: 14:00 - 16:00 Mon/Tue/Wed/Thu - come to talk through your
thinking with the IC and RCA team.

## Milestone 1: Project Inception (5%)

> Elevator Pitch - communicating the ambition and excitement of your project idea.

Choose a topic, explore the context through research, and formulate a problem
statement and "How Might We..." question. Presented as a pre-recorded **3-minute
elevator-pitch micro-presentation**.

### Context, problem and storytelling (who/what/when/where/why/how?)

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| No context or missing important elements. Focus is internal. e.g. team only / stuck in comfort zone. | Some context, but in a vague scenario without a clear need. Focus is limited. e.g. family, friends, colleagues. | Clear context, scenario and need, but focused on only a single stakeholder group. | Rich context, with tangible access to diverse stakeholder groups. |

### People and personas (the actors who bring the experience to life)

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| No real people. | Real, but overly abstracted or lacking authenticity. | Well-defined characters/personas, but poorly integrated into pitch. | Well-defined characters/personas used to drive narrative. |

### Evidence: a range of data that illustrates a real challenge and the potential value of the project

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nothing. No data to back up the premise of the project. | Weak evidence. e.g. Hearsay, assumptions, static survey/interview, generic facts/figures, or data analysed only by a third party. | Strong evidence from real people, gathered via interactive HCD techniques. | Strong evidence, as previous, providing powerful qualitative arguments, linked with well-referenced third-party statistics. |

### Insights and analysis

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overly generic or "cliche" observations. e.g. "time is money". | Specific observations, but lacking authenticity of hypothesis. | Specific and authentic observations, but lacking clear impact or value. | Insightful and empathetic observations, with high impact and potential value. |

### Opportunity statement (what need(s) are you solving for?)

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missing or minimal statement. | High-level, abstract statement overly focused on problem, technology, or lacking depth/detail. | Clear "how might we" statement, but lacking completeness, ambition or benefit to users. | Clear "how might we" statement covering impact for all stakeholder groups. Ambitious and interesting. |

## Milestone 2: Concept Development (5%)

> Milestone Review Session - discussing your project progress and receiving feedback.

Expectations: a visual representation of the mock-ups for your digital touchpoint,
tested with real people to validate ideas through high-quality feedback. Also show
evidence of continuous deployment of the initial "walking-skeleton" prototype.

### Visual representation of digital touchpoint (mock-ups for real people)

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| No physical form. | Generic, abstract, static interface, or unfocused site-map. | Meaningful mock-up, but lacks interaction or core focus. | Meaningful mock-ups with clear interlinked core interaction. |

### How have real people interacted with the paper mock-up?

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| No real or significant interaction. | Only static or high-level feedback. e.g. e-mail, survey, WhatsApp or online message boards. | Interactive feedback gathered, building upon static, but lacking clarity or focus in its presentation. | Rich multidimensional and interactive feedback, presented via relevant excerpts. |

### Quality of feedback and validation of the concept

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nothing that helps guide the design or development of the project's digital touchpoint. | Some surface-level, superficial engagement with users. e.g. focus on font/colour/layout rather than interactions. | Detailed discussion and user engagement with mock-ups or digital touchpoint, but lacking focus or context of use. | Deep discussion with users about how the digital touchpoint would work in reality. Might lead to interaction with additional stakeholder groups. |

### Preparing development process for digital touchpoint

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Git repository set-up. | Functioning CI build system. | Full public deployment. | Functioning CD system. |

### Preparing a walking skeleton for digital touchpoint

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chosen tech-stack for future application development. | Current state is a generic hello-world, todo, map or login application. | Current state is context-specific, but lacks any context-relevant interaction. | Current state prototypes a core context-relevant interaction of your digital touchpoint. |

## Milestone 3: Incremental Development (5%)

> Milestone Review Session - discussing your project progress and receiving feedback.

Expectations: a context-relevant digital touchpoint shown to real users with
high-quality feedback. Also show evidence of agile sprint planning, driven by user
stories, and thin slicing of feature development.

### Sprint planning

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| No clear design or development planning in place. | Evidence of agile workflow (e.g. project board), but development not informed by user stories. | Clear evidence of agile-and-user-centred development in presentation of sprint's planning. | As previous, but current sprint's planning is also clearly framed in wider user journey. |

### Digital touchpoint (designed for real people)

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Generic, abstract, static interface, or unfocused site-map, likely developed from static feedback. | Meaningful design, but lacking context-relevant interactions. | Meaningful design with helpful, context-relevant interactions. | An engaging application, rich with meaningful and usable context-relevant interactions. |

### How have real people interacted with the digital touchpoint

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Only static or high-level feedback. e.g. e-mail, survey, WhatsApp or online message boards. | Interactive feedback gathered, building upon static, but lacking clarity or focus in its presentation. | Rich multidimensional and interactive feedback, presented via relevant excerpts. | Rich multidimensional feedback and application of clear/specific customised HCD data gathering methods in sessions with users. |

### Quality of feedback and validation of the idea

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nothing that helps guide the design or development of the project's digital touchpoint. | Some surface-level, superficial engagement with users. e.g. focus on font/colour/layout rather than interactions. | Detailed discussion and user engagement with digital touchpoint, but lacking focus or context of use. | Feedback provides a clear direction for the digital touchpoint towards being useful and helpful for a wide range of stakeholder groups. |

### Thin slicing development

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| No evidence of thin slicing. i.e. skewed development on Front-End, Back-End, or DB. | Evidence of partial thin slicing or a single thin slice in this sprint's project progress. | Clear evidence of thin slicing, but favouring volume of features over refinement of specific interactions. | Clear evidence of distinct thin slices in ongoing development and refinement of specific interactions. |

## Project Documentation (20%)

> Light-Weight Report - advertise your project and discuss its legal context and your design process.

Prepare a small portfolio of documents detailing specific aspects of your DRP group
project. The bulk of the content should have been developed during the project; the
documentation submission just provides some narrative.

Required documents:

1. **Project Pitch Leaflet (10%)**
2. **Copyright/Legal Issues Report (10%)**
3. **Human Centred Design Techniques Portfolio (80%)**

> *Length note:* with the exception of the HCD Techniques Portfolio, each document
> should be **no more than a single side of A4**. The HCD Techniques Report varies
> in length depending on evidence/support material included, but the **discussion
> itself is limited to a single side of A4**. Use diagrams to convey information
> where possible.

### 1. Project Pitch Leaflet

Create a leaflet that sums up the purpose and use of your digital touchpoint. It
should include a logo, product name, a short description of the value proposition,
and an example screenshot of the digital touchpoint communicating a key user journey
and how it solves a particular problem (highlighting the before and after). You might
also visualise the context of use. The leaflet should generate interest with its
target audience in the context of a tech fair or publicity event.

| Weak (unchecked) | Strong (checked) |
|---|---|
| Missing project name, or name overly generic | Clearly displays good choice of project name |
| Missing, generic or unhelpful screenshot/mock-up | Well chosen and emotive screenshot/mock-up |
| Missing or vague problem statement | Clear and concise problem statement |
| Missing or vague target-audience | Clear and specific target-audience |
| Missing or vague solution proposition | Clear and specific solution proposed |

### 2. Copyright/Legal Issues Report

In a list or table, acknowledge any libraries, code, or pictures used in your final
digital touchpoint that were not written or created by your group. In a short
paragraph, briefly discuss any legal issues to consider if you were to officially
release your digital touchpoint. This document should be suitable for a future
development team to understand the legal constraints within the scope of continuing
work on this project.

| Weak (unchecked) | Strong (checked) |
|---|---|
| Missing or incomplete resource/license list | Clear list of all third-party resources & licenses |
| Resource <-> License links not clear | Resources and their licenses clearly linked |
| License legal implications not understood | License legal implications well-considered |
| Copyright legal implications not understood | Copyright legal implications well-considered |
| Wider legal implications not understood | Wider legal implications well-considered |

### 3. Human Centred Design Techniques Portfolio

The DRP project is a vehicle to explore design methods and tools to inspire new
thinking and develop digital innovation, always aiming to create new and improved
experiences for people. This portfolio should be an engaging, visual representation
of your approach, illustrating your team's use of HCD techniques at each stage,
your reflections on what you learned, and how this contributed to the ongoing
development of your digital touchpoint.

Include the design methods and approaches used throughout the project, with
reflection on how these helped guide your work. Include any completed templates and
tools from the DRP Playbook. Note: we look for **"good use"** of these methods,
rather than the **volume** of materials. Clearly illustrate what you've learned, how
it evolved your thinking in the ultimate design, and the impact on your audience.

Example techniques:

- **AEIOU of Field Research** (Activities, Environments, Interactions, Objects, Users)
  - how this helped you understand what's happening in a particular environment
- **User Personas** (or similar) used for storytelling
- **Stakeholder Maps** (highlighting different user groups)
- **User Journey Maps** (the before experience and after experience)
- **Blueprints** that help visualise the different layers of an experience (people,
  activities, touchpoints, feelings, etc.)
- **The different prototyping techniques** used at each stage of the project
- **Testing Plan Canvas**
- **Cover Story mock-up** (demonstrating the wider impact of your proposition)

#### Rubric: People

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missing | Poorly defined target audience lacking specific focus or well-defined characters. | Generic and non-specific people or personas. | Clear understanding and empathy illustrating the needs/behaviours of the target audience in your chosen context. |
| Missing | Lack of awareness of other stakeholder groups relevant to the chosen context whose needs and behaviours should be considered. | High-level understanding of stakeholder groups, but not enough detail to help with meaningful design decisions. | Clear understanding of relevant stakeholder groups whose needs and behaviours should be considered in your proposition. |

#### Rubric: Current/Future State (what's happening now vs the preferred, future experience)

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missing | Current state not understood, or missing important elements. | Some representation, but lacking in-depth knowledge that connects with your idea. | Design methods used illustrate clear evidence of understanding the current state. |
| Missing | Insights lack authenticity and don't connect with a compelling design solution (future experience). | Insights appear authentic, but fail to resonate with your design solution (future experience). | Insights generated are empathetic and clearly create a high-impact solution. |
| Missing | Generic/high-level opportunity statement that doesn't relate to the scenario explored. | The opportunity statement doesn't link well with the insights that were generated. | Clear opportunity statement considering the needs of all stakeholder groups. |

#### Rubric: Testing and Validation

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missing | Little or no evidence of user feedback impact. | Feedback represented, but no clear connection between the changes made and their impact. | Clear evidence of user feedback leading to a richer and improved experience. |
| Missing | Evolution of digital touchpoint missing or poorly documented. | Clear approach to testing (from concept, to feature, to experience), but lacking authentic connection to the context. | Visual representation of digital touchpoint evolution (from concept, to feature, to seamless and richer experience) showing authentic builds. |
| Missing | No meaningful reflection on the reason for changes in the digital touchpoint. | Some reflections but not representing a consistent approach. | Insightful reflection on what led to distinct changes in the digital touchpoint. |

#### Rubric: Understanding Impact

| Level 0 | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missing | Impact of new digital touchpoint on target audience poorly considered. | Some consideration of impact made, but not fully explored or superficial. | Meaningful evidence, clearly showing a better outcome for the target audience. |
| Missing | Impact on and experience of other stakeholder groups not considered. | The impact created for different stakeholder groups is considered to some degree, but not fully. | Meaningful evidence, clearly leading to an improved experience for all relevant stakeholder groups. |
