Collated feedback from each of your markers

Prioritise Fixing

Things that need urgent attention before next week.

Whilst you are clearly working in an agile way, you seem to be overly focussed on features rather than user needs. You do have user stories in your planning, but these are posed entirely in terms of technical functionality, not in terms of user needs. This is missing the point of working in this way. User stories should not be 1:1 with thin-slices. Try to make it more user focussed next week.

Your user stories are currently too small and feel like they have been written to match individual features rather than to reflect genuine user needs. User stories should come from understanding what a person is trying to achieve, not from the features you have already decided to build. Step back and rewrite them from the user's perspective.

To Improve

Things you should improve moving forward (likely for next week, but may be something more specific), or something you should consider looking into.

Its not always clear where the thin-slice objectives/insights have come from. Is this just what you think, or really what users need? Try to clarify this next week. Good user interviews this week, but maybe need more specific scenarios, context or tasks to carry out next week. Be careful to avoid asking any leading questions in your interviews e.g. "does this interface feel intuitive to you?". Try to use more neutral language next week. The application could use some clearer UI in general, especially around the labelling (make this more call-to-action). Really pay attention to where you users get lost during interviews next week.

Try to synthesise the feedback you receive from users rather than applying it literally to the service. What users ask for and what they actually need are not always the same thing. Look for the underlying need behind each piece of feedback before deciding what to change. Rather than asking users vague questions about what they think of a particular screen or process, try putting them in a real situation. Have you actually tried organising a meetup using your own service? Doing that would reveal the experience not just from the organiser's perspective but also from the voter's side, and would surface friction points that a testing session would never uncover. For the final presentation, make sure your service is clearly connected to your persona and their specific experience throughout. The story of who this person is and why this problem matters to them should be running through everything you show.
